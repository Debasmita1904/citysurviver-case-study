01 — Context

Moving away means starting a search from scratch.

When students move to a new city for college or work, finding a place to stay often means piecing together listings, maps and word of mouth.

CitySurviver is a concept exploring a more organised way to do that: search by location, choose an accommodation type, set practical preferences and read clear property information in one place.

This is a design concept. It is not a launched platform.

03 — Exploratory research

Exploratory survey — 3 respondents.

I conducted an informal exploratory survey with three friends to understand a few experiences and concerns around searching for accommodation away from home. The responses highlighted themes around location accuracy, safety, pricing transparency and the time involved in comparing options. These early observations informed the direction of the CitySurviver concept.

Respondents: 2 college students and 1 recent graduate; 2 from Bhubaneswar and 1 from Manipur. Two had searched for accommodation before; one planned to search in future.

04 — Key observations

Early observations, not conclusions.

01

Location uncertainty

Difficulty finding places near college or work; one respondent saw map listings whose locations had shifted.

→ Location-based search and distance labels
02

Trust and safety

Safety was a concern, and one property looked different from its photos.

→ Verified tags, photos, owner details
03

Pricing transparency

One experience involved undisclosed extra charges; budget mattered.

→ Prominent price and budget filter
04

Time-consuming discovery

Comparing options took time, and the middleman process was frustrating.

→ Comparable result cards, direct contact

08 — The final product

Five screens.

01 · HOME

Home dashboard

The starting point: a greeting, a monthly stipend and remaining balance card, location search, quick shortcuts and available rooms.

  • Budget sits at the top so price stays in view.
  • Verified and Popular badges flag listings.
02 · SEARCH

Search

Location chips, popular nearby areas and browse-by-type tiles with property counts, then recommended listings. Scroll the phone to see it all.

  • Four accommodation types as large, tappable tiles.
  • Listing cards show amenities and metro distance.
03 · RESULTS

Search results

A consistent card layout for scanning: tags, rating, distance, amenities, availability and starting price.

  • Filter count, sort and map controls stay above the list.
  • Availability chips such as "2 Rooms Available".
04 · FILTERS

Filters

Budget range, accommodation type, gender preference, room sharing, amenities and distance, with an option for physically verified properties only.

  • Selected options switch to a dark fill.
  • Apply button shows the match count.
05 · DETAILS

Property details

Photo gallery, rating, owner with call and message actions, distances and amenities, with rent and Contact Owner pinned at the bottom.

  • Primary action always visible.
  • Distances give location context at a glance.

10 — Reflection

What I'd do next.

This project let me explore how accommodation discovery could be structured: location, type, budget and preferences in one flow. The main design challenge was showing many property details without overwhelming the screen, using tags, short amenity lists and a pinned price and action.

The research was small and informal: three friends, no interviews, no testing. It informed direction but validates nothing. Reviewing the screens I also noticed content inconsistencies to fix, such as the Filters screen header reading "Property Detail" and the details screen using different location data from the results.

Next I would run real interviews with students searching for accommodation, then usability-test this flow.
